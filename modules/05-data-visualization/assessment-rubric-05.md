# Module 5: Data & Visualization

## Assessment Rubric — Data & Visualization

Related unit: 5.1 — Canvas Basics — Simple Bar Chart

### Competence mapping (DigComp 2.2)

- 1.2 Evaluating data and information
- 3.1 Developing digital content (visual representations)
- 3.2 Integrating and re-elaborating (from numbers to visuals)
- 5.1 Problem solving (implementing a scaling approach)
- 5.3 Creativity and innovation (design and presentation)

Use the same four performance levels across the module rubrics. Score each criterion from 1–4 and record the evidence observed or collected.

| Criterion | Emerging (1) | Developing (2) | Proficient (3) | Advanced (4) | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data handling and scaling | Values are not mapped consistently; chart sizes misrepresent the data. | Uses a scale, but values or edge cases are handled inconsistently. | Applies a consistent scale so visual values are proportional to the dataset. | Chooses and explains a suitable domain and handles zero or unusually large values. | Dataset, calculation/code, and chart. |
| Chart correctness | Bars overlap, are missing, or do not fit the canvas. | Most bars display, but spacing or sizing has visible issues. | Bars are spaced, sized, and drawn correctly for the dataset. | Layout adapts to changes in canvas size or dataset length. | Rendered chart at two sizes or with changed data. |
| Labels and readability | Title, values, or context labels are missing or hard to read. | Some labels are present but units/context or contrast are unclear. | Title, context, units, and values are legible and meaningful. | Source and labels support interpretation and improve visual hierarchy. | Chart screenshot and label review. |
| Accessibility | No non-visual alternative, or meaning relies on colour alone. | Some accessible features are present, but chart and alternative differ. | Companion HTML table presents the same data; colour is not the only cue. | Adds a useful text summary or annotations and verifies consistency. | Chart-to-table comparison and accessibility check. |
| Reflection and justification | Gives little or no explanation of choices. | Names a choice but gives limited reasoning. | Explains dataset, scale, labels, and one design choice. | Evaluates trade-offs and proposes a specific improvement. | Short written reflection or oral explanation. |
